At  the center of JiJi Township in Nantou County stands the century-old Jiji  Railway Station, a hinoki timber structure built during the Japanese colonial  period. Preserved to this day, it remains a key stop on the JiJi branch line  and a living witness to the history of Taiwan’s railway development.\
The  project site sits immediately beside this historic station. Formerly occupied  by souvenir stalls, the area had fallen into decline due to poor maintenance,  disorganized circulation, and the downturn in tourism brought about by the  pandemic. The JiJi Railway Foothills Market was conceived as an architectural  response to revitalize the town’s tourism industry by creating a more  comfortable and visitor-friendly experience.\
The  overall design takes inspiration from the timber heritage of Jiji Station  while responding to the site’s railway and urban fabric, while introducing  new layers of tourism, commerce, and cultural engagement. With an east–west  linear configuration, the project connects the station’s public facilities  with nearby green corridors, forming a continuous axis that integrates rail  travel and bicycle tourism.\
The  architecture follows the contours of the JiJi mountain landscape and the  station’s form. Low gabled roofs preserve the open skyline, while  cross-laminated timber (CLT) panels form the folded roof system supported by  steel columns. This wide-span structure shapes a diverse and comfortable  semi-outdoor market environment.\
CLT,  used for both the roof and selected wall surfaces, reduces the need for  additional finishing materials and lowers energy consumption. Its inherent  fire resistance properties further minimizes reliance on applied  fireproofing. The prefabricated hybrid system of CLT and steel structures  allows for off-site fabrication and rapid on-site assembly, effectively  shortening the construction period and minimizing disruption to the tourist  area and temporary vendor operations.\
The  overall roof plane slopes gently from north to south, shielding against  southern sunlight while drawing in soft northern daylight. Seemingly  scattered steel columns delicately support the 15 cm-thick CLT roof, their  forms expressed in three types—V-shaped, A-shaped, and vertical—each  integrated according to structural, spatial, and functional needs.\
The  project accommodates 22 vendor units, creating opportunities for local  businesses to settle in. In addition, an 83m2 indoor space has  been established, operated through a public–private partnership model. By  linking public resources with private-sector management, the space fosters  resilient competitiveness and a sustainable cycle of commercial vitality. Beyond  its commercial function, the project aspires to promote sustainable  architecture and integrate with local culture, generating added value in both  tourism development and educational outreach.
