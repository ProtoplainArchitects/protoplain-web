title: "Ta-Ming SHS"
subtitle: "Between Mountain and Water"

山、水、之間。大明高中校舍

The  site is located on the boundary between Taichung City and its foothills, a  transitional zone where urban convenience meets natural beauty. For students  arriving from across the region, it provides an ideal environment where  learning is interwoven with nature. Surrounded by greenery and hydrological  textures, the campus not only offers rich potential for ecological education  but also provides fertile ground for art and experiential learning.\
Within  such a landscape and climate, architecture must respond to natural patterns,  shaping a learning environment that belongs to both students and the land  itself. The design takes “harmonious coexistence of mountain, water, and  people” as its central theme, aiming to create a layered learning  environment. Referencing the vocabulary of the existing campus, the  architecture incorporates mountain and water imagery to realize a vision of  comfort, sustainability, and education combined.\
The  design employs large sloping steel roofs to echo the surrounding mountain  imagery. The air cavity between roof and classrooms reduces solar heat gain  while creating shaded, rain-protected semi-outdoor learning spaces, enabling  teaching activities to flow more freely between indoors and nature.\
The  classroom volumes are arranged with stepped setbacks, forming a layered  mountain-like profile and generating multi-level terraces for outdoor  activities and social exchange. Two central atriums introduce daylight,  airflow, and rainwater, making nature an active participant in the learning  process. Structurally, the building adopts a lightweight system, with locally  reduced beam depths and beamless spans in classrooms to enhance openness and  comfort.\
The  floor plan is designed around a central courtyard, shaping a cohesive campus  core that strengthens students’ sense of belonging and interaction. On this  land between mountain and water, the architecture assumes a humble  posture—allowing wind and light to flow through its spaces and embodying the  educational belief in coexistence and co-learning between people and nature.\
We  have built a mountain of learning for the children.\
The  courtyard is left open, letting the wind pass through.\
The  atriums gather light, shadow, and mist.\
The  spaces breathe with the turning of the seasons.\
This  is more than a classroom.\
It  is where nature and knowledge coexist,\
a  place where teaching and learning grow in harmony with the mountains, rivers,  and waters that surround it.
