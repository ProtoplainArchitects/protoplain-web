森林的樹蔭。沐竹軒

Bamboo  arcs in a gentle half-moon curve, lifting the roof as if in quiet embrace.\
Here,  wind, light, bamboo, and trees converge in sensory harmony,\
allowing  visitors to feel immersed in the forest’s shade.\
Located  beside the shared rest platform and along the shortcut between the Visitor  Center and the second parking lot, the restroom facility is one of the most  frequently used in the area. Yet issues persist: surrounding mechanical  equipment is visually intrusive, privacy is compromised by open sightlines  from adjacent paths, and the lack of accessible features and buffer zones  reduces comfort and inclusivity.\
The  design responds while retaining the existing structure. By opening the roof,  extending semi-outdoor space, refining the façade, and adding an accessible  restroom, the intervention creates an environment that is practical,  comfortable, and private. Behind the restroom facility, the shortcut path  descends from higher ground, exposing restroom users to direct sightlines. To  address this, the new single-slope roof rises in front and lowers at the  back, shielding views from above.\
At  eye level, circular openings punctuate the façade, paired with an extended  eave. These allow users to frame the surrounding greenery while preventing  passersby from peering inward. At the restroom entrances, doors are removed  and portions of wall cut away, reinforced with slender steel columns,  creating visual porosity that connects interior space to the surrounding  landscape.
