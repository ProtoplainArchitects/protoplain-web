A  Cross-Disciplinary Interpretation of Commercial Space\
"Through  similarities and differences, new knowledge and vitality emerge."\
What  defines the ideal workplace? Is it simply about maximizing productivity? Or  should it go beyond work itself—breaking down interpersonal barriers to  foster the flow of knowledge, ideas, and shared value?\
When  the balance between work and life must be reimagined, how should architecture  respond?\
Around  expansive office and service spaces, a variety of green spaces are integrated  at different scales. These soften the work atmosphere, allowing nature to  weave into daily routines.  In response  to the varying conditions of the south, west, and north orientations, the  design develops tailored daylighting and ventilation strategies. On the south  side, staggered deep balconies not only provide shading and outdoor respite  but also create layered encounters with different parts of the tree canopy.  On the west side, low-set windows preserve outward views while hedges block  the harsh afternoon sun. On the north side, expansive glazing brings in  steady, glare-free daylight throughout the day.\
Modern  workplaces demand more than air, light, and greenery. In industries built on  intensive specialization, individuals often feel compressed—minuscule, yet  carrying immense pressure. To respond, a curved wall is introduced along the  circulation leading to service areas. This gesture instantly shifts space and  time, offering a momentary retreat. Here, one can step away from work, pause,  and breathe—finding a narrow seam of relief in the day.\
A  Nook not only addresses the functional needs of its tenants, but also opens  new possibilities for how people gather and connect. On the ground floor, its  flexible layout accommodates everything from performance celebrations to  industry forums and cross-regional meetings. The Event Space (Rippling)  transforms A Nook into a versatile communal venue, with parts of the building  deliberately opened to the public. Here, art, exhibitions, and performances  take place—breaking down the external walls not just passively, but through  active creation of new encounters. In doing so, A Nook fosters emotional  resonance and flow between people, neighbors, and the surrounding city.
