---
title: "Liu House in Taichung"
subtitle: "The Intersection of Traffic"
---

十字路口的交通。臺中劉宅

The  Liu House stands at a corner site near Taichung’s rear railway station, at  the intersection of a 30-meter arterial road and a 20-meter secondary street  — a typical setting for urban shophouses in Taiwan. On this compact,  irregularly-shaped site, the design confronts challenges of direct  southeastern sunlight, constant traffic noise, and the restless energy of the  city.\
Our  response is a buffering strategy: redefining the building envelope to  mitigate both natural (sunlight) and artificial (traffic and urban) impacts  on the home.\
The  project revisits the shophouse vocabulary of Taichung in the 1960s and 1970s,  when concrete sunshades were common. Yet those earlier designs often lacked  precision in addressing solar orientation.\
Here,  horizontal and vertical shading panels reinterpret and refine this language,  fulfilling three roles:\
1.  Diminishing the turbulence of traffic and urban noise.\
2.  Moderating harsh southeastern summer sunlight, softening natural illumination  indoors.\
3.  Extending inward as window seats that merge with the living space,  transforming shading into features that are both functional and  contemplative.

The  vertical circulation core is conceived as a conduit of light. Skylights and a  translucent steel stair draw daylight from the fifth floor down to the  second, ensuring comfortable illumination throughout the day. In tandem,  solar heat and north-facing windows generate cross-ventilation, establishing  a microclimate that naturally lifts and expels warm air.\
The  rooftop level is envisioned as another vital living space, centered around a  multifunctional island that serves as a platform for daily tasks such as  folding clothes and, by evening, transforms into a lounge for the roof  garden. This outdoor landscape garden allows the retired couple to immerse  themselves in the restorative power of plants, making everyday life a  dialogue with nature.\
For  the homeowners—both in their seventies—the interior layout respects  established living habits while addressing the needs of aging in place. By  positioning the structural elements and circulation to either side, the  central space remains column-free, creating a flexible, expansive area for  shared activities. This openness encourages adaptability and imagination,  while the interplay of vertical and horizontal fins along the façade filters  sunlight and integrates harmoniously with the interior. The result is a  residence where natural light softens and balances, cultivating a home that  is both practical and in harmony.
